🛠️ Offline IoT Development Process

A step-by-step journey from initial trials to the final offline solution.

Step 01Initial Trial

Used an external antenna to increase received WiFi signal strength. Improvement was minimal in real environments, so this approach was not adopted as the main solution.

Step 02ESP‑NOW

Explored ESP‑NOW for direct device-to-device communication. Hardware cost and long‑term connectivity stability remained concerns, limiting suitability for this project.

Step 03Bluetooth

Investigated ESP Bluetooth with MIT App Inventor. The approach worked in principle but introduced unnecessary complexity for web-based interfaces and future expansion.

Step 04Final Choice

Final solution: ESP32 operates as its own Access Point and local web server, enabling reliable offline IoT control independent of any external WiFi infrastructure.

🌐 Hybrid IoT

Seamless control via both Blynk cloud and local ESP32 web interface.

🔥 Automatic Heating Cutoff

The system automatically disables heating when the glove's internal temperature exceeds the defined cutoff limit, protecting the patient from overheating during therapy.


The RGB indicator transitions from yellow to red as temperature approaches the limit. The threshold is fully adjustable via both IoT interfaces to suit individual patient tolerance.

💪 Adjustable Strength Level

Three levels of pump duty cycle for progressive therapy intensity.

⚡ L1: 33% Duty Cycle — gentle inflation for early-stage or sensitive patients.

⚡ L2: 50% Duty Cycle — moderate intensity for progressive strengthening.

⚡ L3: 100% Duty Cycle — maximum assistive movement to challenge hand muscles.

🔄 Adjustable Pump Cycle Times

Customizable inflation and deflation durations to suit therapy goals.

Therapists can tune inflation/deflation timing to match patient endurance and rehabilitation goals.

👥 About
The team behind StrokeEase.
IDP — Integrated Design Project
Group 10
Faculty of Electronic Engineering & Technology · UniMAP · 2025

StrokeEase is an Integrated Design Project developed by Group 10 at Universiti Malaysia Perlis (UniMAP). The project addresses the growing need for accessible and affordable stroke rehabilitation tools. By combining pneumatic actuation, heat therapy, vibration feedback and hybrid IoT control, StrokeEase delivers a multi-modal rehabilitation glove that empowers both patients and clinicians with real-time monitoring and adaptive therapy settings.
